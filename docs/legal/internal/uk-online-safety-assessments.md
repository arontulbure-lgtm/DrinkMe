# UK Online Safety Act 2023 – assessments (internal)

**Applies only if the UK is in scope** (decision D-03). The Act covers user-to-user services with "links with the UK" (a significant number of UK users, the UK as a target market, or material risk of significant harm to UK users) – OSA s.4(5)–(6). VERIFY with a UK lawyer.

Timeline found on 2026-11-12 (law-firm summaries of Ofcom's timetable; Ofcom page "Important dates for online safety compliance"): illegal-content risk assessments due 16 March 2025 for existing services; first children's access assessment due 16 April 2025; children's risk assessments due 24 July 2025. A **new** service must complete these before it launches to UK users (VERIFY: Ofcom guidance says within 3 months of the service starting – check). Sources: Ofcom, https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/important-dates-for-online-safety-compliance ; Mayer Brown, "The Online Safety Act Enters Phase 2" (Aug 2025).

## 2. Children's access assessment (OSA s.35–36)
A service is "possible for children to access" unless it uses age verification or age estimation that is **highly effective** so that children are not normally able to access it (s.35(2)–(3), Ofcom Guidance on highly effective age assurance – VERIFY). DrinkMe uses a **self-declared** "I am 18 or older" confirmation, which Ofcom does not treat as highly effective. **Conclusion: if the UK is in scope, DrinkMe is likely treated as possible for children to access; the child user condition then depends on numbers/attraction (s.35(3)–(4)) – likely met conservatively because alcohol and social content may attract under-18s.** Consequences: children's risk assessment (s.11) and safety duties.

Options (decision D-03): (a) do not distribute in the UK at launch; (b) add highly effective age assurance for UK users (e.g. a third-party age-estimation or the stores' age signals) and record why children cannot normally access; (c) accept the children's duties.
